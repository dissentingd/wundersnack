About wundersnack

The natural world is full of wonder most of us never get to see: a mushroom that glows, a single cell with a glass skeleton, a star orbiting a black hole. wundersnack is a quick break to meet some of it.

No accounts, no likes, no comments, no tracking. Just look for a minute and feel a bit better.

Gross, but cool. Slime molds and creepy-crawlies (flies, spiders, worms and the like) are fascinating but not for everyone, so they're hidden unless you tap the bug button at the top.

A snack, not a meal. After five minutes we suggest a break. You can have one more snack; after that, wundersnack rests for an hour. It's meant to leave you feeling better, not to keep you scrolling.

Helped? If you tap a thumb, we count one anonymous up or down for that item (nothing about you or your device is sent), so we can find and show more of what actually helps. What you've seen and how you voted is remembered only in this browser, so you keep meeting new things.

Every picture and video is shown with its creator's credit, license and a link to where it lives. (Videos from Wikimedia Commons are converted once into a format every phone can play.) Sources: iNaturalist observers, NASA, ESO, Wikimedia Commons (including Wiki Loves Earth winners and Picture of the Year finalists), and hand-picked videos embedded from their original channels. Facts come from Wikipedia (CC BY-SA). "Did you know?" lines and fact cards are checked by hand, each with a link to its source; fact-card footage carries its own credit.
